**A statement descriptor is a clue, not a complete purchase record.** Swipey's [current billing FAQ](https://swipey.ai/faq/hearts-billing/how-does-swipey-ai-appear-on-my-bank-statement) identifies SWIPEY.AI and INVAI LTD as possible labels. It says the payment provider affects which appears.

This matters because the provider's [October 12 billing article](https://swipey.ai/blog/ai-companion-bank-statement) uses broader neutral-name wording. Do not turn that general wording into a guarantee that the service name can never appear.

## Match the transaction, not only the company

Compare date, amount, currency, payment method and receipt reference. Preserve whether the entry is pending or posted. A current plan price viewed later is not a substitute for the accepted terms on an earlier purchase.

For an invented example, a 20-unit statement entry and a 20-unit receipt are a possible match. If their dates, currency or payment reference differ, the numerical match alone does not settle it. Mark unresolved fields rather than forcing the records to agree.

## The name does not identify every product

The FAQ says the possible descriptors can cover several purchase categories. Do not infer from INVAI LTD alone that the entry must be a subscription renewal rather than another recorded purchase.

Separate the product question from the authorization question. Even if a merchant is correctly identified, that does not establish who initiated the payment or whether the cardholder allowed it.

## Keep a private reconciliation record

Save the statement entry and relevant receipt privately, with unnecessary financial details redacted from any copy sent to support. Reach the provider independently from its official site; do not use an unsolicited message as proof that a contact represents the merchant.

Ask a bounded question: does this transaction reference belong to this account, and which purchase does it represent? No support helper needs a password, sign-in code or full card number to understand that question.

## Suspected unauthorized use is a separate issue

Do not let a merchant's reassuring descriptor explanation replace your issuer's process for a charge you did not authorize. The [US CFPB guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-dispute-a-charge-on-my-credit-card-bill-en-61/) advises contacting the card company promptly and preserving records. Applicable procedures depend on the card and jurisdiction; this guide does not decide an individual dispute.

If the entry is recognized and the objective is stopping renewal, verify the subscription's resulting state separately. A bank descriptor, a cancellation confirmation and a refund decision are three different records. Matching them accurately is more useful than treating a familiar company name as proof that every question is resolved.
